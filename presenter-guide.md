# GTP ECO — 60-minute presenter guide

Audience: small-business owners. Example: **Amina’s Kitchen**, a fictional Johannesburg catering business. Keep the example clearly illustrative. No sales or demographic figures are real research. The on-screen experience is the audience view. Each scene scrolls slowly while you speak and holds before the next scene. Press Next when ready. Your wheel, touch or scroll keys take over temporarily. Scene 2 lets you click the ECO stages and holds the choice until Next. The footer arrows and Overview jump between numbered scenes. Use Cinema for full screen, Notes for speaker prompts and Pause scroll to halt automatic and decorative movement. Reduced-motion settings keep content and controls available without automatic motion. On phones, visitors can also scroll naturally.

## Presenter notes need a second screen

1. Before the session (2 minutes): set the laptop to **extend** the display, not mirror it. Put the deck on the projector and keep the laptop screen for yourself.
2. The Notes panel opens over the right half of the slide (at 1366 px wide it covers the hero). On a mirrored or single screen the room sees your notes, so on one screen keep Notes closed and read from a printed copy of this guide instead.
3. The deck keeps moving while Notes is open, so opening it on your own screen never stops the scene.

## Session rhythm

Keep this a working session. Use a **five-minute overview** (scenes 1–2), then alternate a short explanation with a visible example and a decision participants make for their own business. Amina’s fictional business gives everyone the same orders, ad, caption, quote request and result to inspect. Take a show of hands or one short answer. Move on before the discussion crowds out the next demonstration.

**Participation rule:** give people 20–40 seconds to write or decide, take one or two answers, then continue. For owners without records, have them write a hypothesis and the next question to ask a customer. The final seven-day commitment turns the workshop into a test they can actually run.

Total: **60 minutes**. If behind schedule, shorten the technical detail in scene 7 before removing an audience exercise. These on-screen interactions are local teaching demonstrations. They do not call an AI model or submit visitor information.

## Hands-on exercise: customer evidence

Ask owners to review their last ten orders or inquiries. Record what they bought, occasion, area, discovery source, outcome and repeat purchase where lawfully available. Count the patterns, then ask future customers who decided and what mattered. Do not upload identifiable customer records to an AI service. If the owner has little data, label the segment as a hypothesis and plan three customer conversations. Use age, gender or area only when the data is lawful, relevant and complete enough to guide a decision. Race is special personal information in South Africa. Do not infer it. The method and sources are in [persona-research.md](experience/persona-research.md).

## Channel decision

Give each candidate channel a simple 1–3 score for (1) customer presence, (2) buying intent, (3) content effort they can sustain, and (4) ability to respond. Discuss the trade-off. The score is a decision aid, not a universal platform ranking. Amina’s six office-meeting orders suggest a question people may search locally, but the buyer role is unknown. Start with the illustrated Search/Maps answer-to-quote test, ask each qualified lead how they found the business, and retain that channel only if inquiries and sales support it. Choose one primary discovery channel and one inquiry route for a 30-day test.

## AI demonstration prompts

1. **Customer hypothesis:** “Using only these anonymized counts, identify patterns, alternative explanations and what data is missing. Do not invent demographics.”
2. **Content:** “Our customer asks [question]. Suggest five ways to answer it: short video, carousel, FAQ, product description and WhatsApp reply. Each must have one next action.”
3. **Voice:** “Our brand is [three traits]. Here are two genuine examples we wrote: [examples]. Rewrite this draft to match the examples. List claims I must verify.”
4. **30/60/90:** “Use the observed page and query data below. Propose priorities at 30, 60 and 90 days. Mark ideas without source data as hypotheses and name the measure for each action.”

Keep the human edit visible. Check prices, availability, service areas, testimonials, product claims and visual rights before publication.

## Website practice to demonstrate

- **SEO:** make useful pages accessible and understandable to search engines and people. Review titles, main headings, internal links, speed, mobile layout, local facts and clear next actions.
- **AEO:** make pages answer real questions directly with context and evidence. It is a content practice, not a separate guaranteed ranking mechanism.
- **GEO/AI visibility:** check whether a sample of relevant AI answer prompts names or cites the business, recording the engine and prompt, plus when and where it was tested. Open any cited result. A sample cannot establish comprehensive visibility.
- Start keyword work with [Search Console queries and pages](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/10268906?hl=en) when the owner has access. Check technical discoverability and sitemaps with [Search Console](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-console-start?hl=en), mobile/desktop experience with [PageSpeed Insights](https://developers.google.com/speed/docs/insights/v5/about), and relevant structured data with the [Rich Results Test](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data). A third-party keyword/backlink index is needed before reporting search volume or backlink coverage.
- Use Amina’s Kitchen for a worked decision: “meeting lunches Johannesburg” is a phrase to validate against Search Console or customer questions, then map to one service page with a quote action. Weeks 1–4: review questions/queries, choose the phrase, improve the page, publish one FAQ. Weeks 5–8: publish two useful answers with sources, link them to the offer, test the quote route on a phone. Weeks 9–12: compare qualified inquiries by page and source, refresh weak answers and plan the next quarter. Do not describe the example phrase as measured demand.
